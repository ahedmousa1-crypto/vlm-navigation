01 Idea

NaviTrace measures whether a model can turn an instruction into a walking path through a photo.

Note 1The task

One photo. One instruction. One path.

NaviTrace gives a model a real photo and an instruction, and scores the path it draws against one drawn by a human expert. The task has two parts: locating the goal, and reading the space: where the sidewalk is, which way the crosswalk goes, what stands in the way.

Note 2Our approach

Build the city. Compute the walk.

Path labels are usually drawn by hand. Here, towns are built in 3D, a camera is placed at eye height, and the route is computed from the scene itself, so every path follows from the geometry. An open 8B model trained on these scenes alone scored 36.9 on real photos, up from −28.3 before training.

No hand labels

02 Method

Four steps turn a procedural town into training data.

How a scene is made4 steps
1

Build a town

Streets, squares, parks, stairs, water, people and cafes, generated in 3D by scripts. Every surface is tagged.

2

Place a camera

At eye height on a sidewalk, square or park path, looking where a pedestrian would. 5,373 cameras.

3

Plan the walk

A route planner works out the path a person would take: stay on the sidewalk, cross roads only at crossings, walk around people and obstacles, use stairs or a ramp where needed.

4

Render and label

The image, a per-pixel class mask, the task in plain English, the path in image pixels.

Tracely by Ahed Mousa, 2026. Sample format, prompts and categories follow NaviTrace (Windecker et al., 2025).

Built with Blender, Qwen3-VL, PEFT and Modal. Poly Haven skies and textures, Quaternius animal meshes (CC0). Paths on the scene cards are drawn for illustration; dataset images are clean.
